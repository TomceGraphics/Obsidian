# Data quality assurance

The ongoing practice of ensuring data is **accurate, complete, consistent, timely, and fit for purpose**.

## The five dimensions of data quality
| Dimension | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Accuracy | Is the data correct? |
| Completeness | Is anything missing? |
| Consistency | Does it match across systems? |
| Timeliness | Is it up to date? |
| Relevance | Is it the right data for the purpose? |

## Why poor data quality is costly
"Garbage in, garbage out" — bad source data produces misleading reports, wrong predictions, and poor decisions. Studies estimate data quality issues cost organizations significant revenue annually.

## Common causes of poor quality
- Manual data entry errors
- System migrations that corrupt or lose records
- No single source of truth (same entity stored differently across systems)
- Outdated records not purged

## Relationship to governance
[[Data Governance]] defines the *standards*. Data Quality Assurance is the *process* of measuring and improving data against those standards.
