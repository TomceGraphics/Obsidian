# MIS Foundations: From Builder Logic to Textbook Terms

This document tracks the "First Principles" we've derived and maps them to the formal terminology found in your 6 chapters.

## Chapter 1: Information Systems in Global Business Today

- **The "Living" System:** I identified that an IS isn't just code; it involves people and feedback.
    - _Textbook Term:_ [[Sociotechnical Systems]]. The idea that optimal performance is achieved by jointly optimizing both the social (people) and technical (machines) systems.

- **The 4-Action Loop:** IPOF model.
    - _Textbook Term:_ [[Information Processing Cycle]]. Specifically, **Input, Processing, Output, and Feedback**.

- **Doing things better/faster:** Your goal for the QR system.
    - _Textbook Term:_ [[Operational Excellence]]. Using IS to achieve higher levels of efficiency and productivity.


## Chapter 2: Global E-Business and Collaboration

- **The Ladder of Problems:** You noticed a Cashier, a Manager, and a CEO all need different things from the same system.
    - _Textbook Term:_ [[Hierarchical Information Systems]].
    - _Term:_ [[TPS (Transaction Processing System)]] - The "Fast/Snappy" cashier view.
    - _Term:_ [[MIS (Management Information Systems)]] - The "Scam-catching" manager view (summary reports).
    - _Term:_ [[DSS/ESS (Decision/Executive Support Systems)]] - The "Strategy" admin panel for the CEO.

- **Moving the work to the customer:** Your QR system lets the customer order directly.
    - _Textbook Term:_ **Customer and Supplier Intimacy**. Using systems to create a deep tie with customers that makes them prefer your business.

- **The "One Size Doesn't Fit All":** humans are the wildcards in systems.
    - _Textbook Term:_ [[Strategic Alignment]]. The degree to which an information system supports and is supported by the organization's strategy and people.
    If two guys in Ohrid have the same code, but one restaurant has a staff that hates the app and the other has a staff that uses the data to give better service, the second restaurant wins every time. This is what the textbook calls **Strategic Alignment**.

## Chapter 3: Information Systems, Organizations, and Strategy
*the logic of this chapter is Competitive Advantage*

- **The "Why stick with me?" factor:** You realized that once an owner has their menu, QR codes, and sales history in your app, they won't leave for a $10 discount.
    - _Textbook Term:_ **Switching Costs**. The cost (time, money, effort) a customer incurs when changing from one supplier/product to a competitor.

-  **The Data Moat:** You suggested storing data on your servers so it's not "easy peasy" to migrate.
	 _Textbook Term:_ **Lock-in Strategy**. Designing a system that makes customers dependent on your specific platform.

- **The "Other Guy in Ohrid":** The person trying to copy your features.
    - _Textbook Term:_ **Porter’s Competitive Forces Model**. Specifically: **New Market Entrants** and **Intensity of Rivalry**.

**creating high Switching Costs**
making it a "hassle" for the owner to leave because you have their 50 QR codes, their menu, and their history—is formally called **creating high Switching Costs**. If you purposefully design the system so the data is hard to move, you are building a **Moat** around your business.
